What is Experimental Probability?

Experimental probability is determined by conducting an experiment and recording the outcomes. It is calculated by dividing the number of times an event occurs by the total number of trials in the experiment. Unlike theoretical probability, which is based on possible outcomes, experimental probability is based on actual outcomes.

Conclusion

Theoretical probability gives us a way to understand the chances of different events happening before they occur. By dividing the number of ways an event can happen by the total number of possible outcomes, we can find the probability of that event. This concept is a powerful tool in mathematics, allowing us to make informed predictions and decisions based on the likelihood of various outcomes. Experimental probability provides insight into how probability plays out in real-world situations, while theoretical probability offers a baseline expectation. By understanding both, we can better analyze and predict outcomes in various scenarios.
