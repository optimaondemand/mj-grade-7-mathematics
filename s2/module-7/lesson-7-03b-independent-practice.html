Objective:

Your mission is to design, simulate, and analyze a simple probability experiment. This project will help you understand the difference between theoretical and experimental probabilities through hands-on experience.


Instructions:

Part 1: Designing Your Experiment

  1. Choose Your Experiment: Decide on a probability experiment. It could be flipping a coin, rolling dice, spinning a spinner, drawing a marble out of a bag, or picking a card.
  2. Select a Simulation Tool: Use one of the following websites for your simulation:
    • GeoGebra: https://www.geogebra.org/m/UsoH4eNl
    • Shodor: http://www.shodor.org/interactivate/activities/Marbles/
    • NCTM: https://www.nctm.org/adjustablespinner/
  3. Define Your Experiment: Write a brief description including:
    • The aim of the experiment.
    • The event(s) you are investigating.
    • Why you chose this experiment.
    • If you choose to use the simulation website to represent an experiment that is not marbles, dice, or spinner, then explain what the parts represent.
      • For example; you could use the spinner to simulate drawing a numbered card from a given set of cards. Each color on the spinner would represent the numbered card.

Part 2: Simulating and Recording Results

  1. Conduct Your Experiment: Use your chosen website to simulate the experiment at least 100 times for reliable data. You can do more trials if you'd like. You can also do more than one simulation by starting with 100 and conducting more with more trials to show long-run frequency.
  2. Record Your Results: Create a frequency table in your document, recording the outcome of each trial.

Part 3: Calculating Probabilities

  1. Theoretical Probabilities: Calculate the likelihood of each outcome based on the sample space.
    • Refer back to lesson 7.02b Defining and Determining Theoretical Probability.
  2. Experimental Probabilities: Determine the experimental probability of each outcome from your frequency table.
    • Refer back to lesson Lesson 7.02c Defining and Determining Experimental Probability

Part 4: Analysis and Conclusion

  1. Compare Probabilities: In a paragraph, compare your theoretical and experimental probabilities. Discuss potential reasons for any discrepancies, such as the Law of Large Numbers and randomness.
    • Refer back to lesson Lesson 7.03a Comparing Theoretical and Experimental Probabilities
  2. Summary: Reflect on what your experiment shows about probability and how the simulation helped or challenged your understanding.


Presentation of Your Project

  • Using Engage:

    • Create a snapshot detailing your experiment's design, frequency table, and calculations.
    • Record a myrec explaining your project, including your findings and analysis.
    • Save and share your myrec and snapshot with your teacher.

  • Using Microsoft or Google Programs:

    • Document your experiment, including the design, frequency table, theoretical and experimental probabilities, and your analysis.
    • Prepare a presentation to visually share your project's parts. Include images, tables, or screenshots from your simulation.
    • Optionally, use a screen recording tool (e.g., Canvas Studio) to create a video presentation of your project, similar to a myrec.


Submission Guidelines:

If you create a snapshot and myrec, get your teacher's Engage Username to share the snapshot and myrec with them. Be sure to save your snapshot and myrec to your cloud when working in Engage. Go to the Engage Web portal https://app.engagevr.io/login to share your snapshot and myrec with your teacher.

If you used Microsoft, Google, or an online Presentation site to create your project, and created a video presentation, then use the Media Recordings or File Upload options below to upload your project to this assignment and submit it for your teacher to review.

Be sure that your files are labeled clearly with your Name and Class period to assist your teacher with grading.


Tips for Success:

  • Be clear and precise in your experiment design and description.
  • Ensure accuracy when recording your data and performing calculations.
  • Reflect thoughtfully on the comparison between theoretical and experimental probabilities.

This project is an opportunity to explore probability in a hands-on manner, enhancing your understanding through practical application and analysis. Good luck, and enjoy your exploration into the world of probability!
