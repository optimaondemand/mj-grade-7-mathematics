📚 VOCABULARY

Event: Refers to any outcome or set of outcomes of a probability experiment. In simpler terms, it's anything that can happen in a situation involving chance. For example, in a dice-rolling experiment, rolling a number greater than 4 is an event (which includes rolling a 5 or a 6).

Likelihood: A term used to describe how probable it is that an event will occur. It can be thought of as the chance or odds that a specific outcome will happen, based on the possible outcomes. Likelihood can be expressed in various ways, such as words ("unlikely," "likely"), fractions, percentages, or decimals. For instance, the likelihood of flipping a coin and landing on heads is 50%.

Probability: A numerical measure of how likely an event is to occur. It ranges from 0 to 1, where 0 means the event is impossible (it cannot happen), and 1 means the event is certain (it will definitely happen). Probability can also be expressed as a percentage, with 0% being impossible and 100% being certain.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore comparing the likelihood of events happening in different situations. We have previously explored finding the likelihood of an event happening. Comparing the probability's likelihood will help us to understand which event is most likely or least likely to happen. What do we have a better chance of doing? Understanding this can help us make decisions in real life when faced with options that are based on chance.

Let's begin by reviewing the probability scale. Then we will watch a quick video on comparing some chance events and their likelihood of happening.

Probability Scale

The probability of an event tells us how likely it is to happen and is usually expressed as a percentage (%). Here’s what each term means:

  • Impossible (0%): The event cannot happen.
  • Unlikely (<50%): The event is not expected to happen most of the time.
  • As Likely as Unlikely (50%): The event has an equal chance of happening or not happening.
  • Likely (>50%): The event is expected to happen most of the time.
  • Certain (100%): The event will definitely happen.


Probability Notation

Probability of an event occurring is expressed in the following notation P(event). For example, the probability of rolling a 5 on a standard six-sided dice is written as P(rolling a 5).

A standard 6-sided dice has 6 faces each numbered 1-6. So there is one 5 out of 6 numbers on the dice. This would be a low percentage of chance to roll a 5.


Now, let's review some examples.

Example 1

Imagine a spinner divided into equal parts, each section contains a color. The chance of the spinner landing on any given section is determined by the size of the section relative or the number of sections containing that color to the whole spinner.

Suppose you have a spinner divided into 6 equal sections: there are 2 brown sections, 3 purple sections, and 1 blue section.

Which color has the greatest likelihood of the spinner landing on?

Solution:

  • There are 3 purple sections which is more than any of the other colors.
  • Purple has the greatest likelihood

Which color has the least likelihood of the spinner landing on?

  • Since there is only one blue section, this will be the least likely color for the spinner to land on

Do any of the colors have an almost equal likelihood?

  • Brown and purple have almost the same number of sections and could have a close to equal likelihood of happening.
  • Purple represents almost half the circle. So, it is definitely the most possible. However, brown would be the next most likely.


Example 2

Now let's look at a weather forecast for a week and the chance of rain on those days.

Which day has the least likely chance of rain?

Solution:

  • Since Thursday is showing a 0% chance of rain, this day has the least likely chance.
    • In weather, you never want to say it is impossible for rain to happen when there is a 0% chance since weather can be unpredictable.

Which day has the greatest likelihood of rain?

  • Tuesday has the greatest likelihood since it has the highest percent chance of rain.
    • Sunday and Tuesday are very close in percentage. So, one could say that Sunday might also have the highest likelihood of rain.

Which days have a close or almost equal chance of rain?

  • Friday, Sunday, and Tuesday are all relatively close in percent chance of rain and could have close to equal likelihood of rain.

There are many questions one could ask and ways of comparing the weather forecast of rain using an image like this. One could explore what days it might be best to go to the park or to the movies based on the probability or chance of rain happening. Comparing probabilities or chance events like this is a helpful life skill when making decisions that can be affected by the chance of an event happening.


Conclusion

Understanding how to calculate and compare probabilities helps you make informed predictions about outcomes. Remember, the key terms (impossible, unlikely, as likely as unlikely, likely, and certain) help you quickly assess how probable an event is. Keep practicing with different spinners or scenarios to become more comfortable with these concepts!
