Understanding statistical measures and what they mean to a set of data is an important skill.


What are the Measures of Center?

Measures of center give us a single value that represents the middle or typical value of a data set. The three main measures of center are:

  1. Mean: The average of all the values.
  2. Median: The middle value when the data is arranged in order.
  3. Mode: The most frequently occurring value.


Mean

  • Calculation: 
    1. Find the sum of all the values in the set of data.
    2. Divide the sum by the number of values in the set.
  • Interpretation:
    • The mean gives you a general idea of where the center of the data lies.
    • It is best used when the data is symmetrically distributed and has no outliers, as outliers can skew the mean.
    • It is useful for understanding the overall level of a variable in the data set, like the average score, average temperature, etc.

Median

  • Calculation: 
    1. Arrange the values in order for the set of data.
    2. Find the middle value.
    3. If there are two values in the middle, add and divide by 2.
  • Interpretation:
    • The median divides the data set into two equal halves.
    • It is less affected by outliers or skewed data compared to the mean.
    • The median is a good indicator of the central tendency, especially in skewed distributions, as it represents a value that is central to the data set.

Mode

  • Calculation: 
    1. Find the most frequent value.
    2. There can be more than one mode or no mode at all.
  • Interpretation:
    • The mode indicates the most common value or category in the data set.
    • It is particularly useful for categorical data where mean and median may not be applicable.
    • In a data set with a clear mode (or modes), it highlights the value(s) that occur most often, which can be significant in understanding trends or preferences.


Example 1

A teacher records the number of students present each day over two weeks. The data set shows the number of students present for the 10 school days.

Find and interpret the mean, median, and mode for the data set. Round to the nearest hundredth if necessary.

  • To find the mean, add all the values.
  • Now, divide by the number of values; 14.

Mean Attendance = 23.6 students

Interpretation: The average attendance is around 23.6 students or between 23 and 24 students.

  • To find the median, order the values.
  • Now, find the middle number.
  • Since there are 2 numbers in the middle, we need to add them and divide by 2.

Median Attendance = 23.5 students

Interpretation: The typical or central number of students in attendance is about 23.5 students. The median attendance suggests that on at least half of the days, approximately 23.5 students were present.

Find and interpret the mode for the data set.

  • Look for the number that appears most frequently.
    • 22 appears 3 times

Mode Attendance = 22 students

Interpretation: The most common number of students in attendance is 22 students. This shows a trend that typically or most often there are around 22 students in attendance.



What are the Measures of Variation?

Measures of variation are statistical tools used to describe the spread or dispersion within a set of data. The three main measures of variation are:

  1. Range: The range gives a basic idea of the spread of the data. The difference between the highest and lowest values in a set of data.
  2. Interquartile Range (IQR): The IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of a data set. It is the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1).

Range

  • Calculation: Range = Maximum Value - Minimum Value
  • Interpretation:
    • The range gives a basic idea of the spread of the data.
    • A large range indicates a wide spread between the smallest and largest values, suggesting high variability.
    • A small range suggests that the data points are more closely clustered together.


Interquartile Range (IQR)

  • Calculation:
    1. Arrange the data in ascending order.
    2. Find the median (the second quartile, Q2), which divides the data set into two halves.
    3. The first quartile (Q1) is the median of the lower half, and the third quartile (Q3) is the median of the upper half.
    4. IQR = Q3 - Q1
  • Interpretation:
    • The IQR focuses on the central portion of the data and is less influenced by outliers or extreme values.
    • A larger IQR indicates more variability within the middle range of the data.
    • A smaller IQR suggests that the middle values are more closely grouped together.


Example 2

A biologist measures the heights of trees in a forest. The data set shown represents the heights, in feet, of the measured trees.

Find and interpret the range and interquartile range (IQR) for the data set.

  • To find the range, we will first identify the minimum and the maximum of the data set.
    • Minimum = 50
    • Maximum = 95
  • Subtract the minimum from the maximum to find the range.

Range of Heights = 45 feet

Interpretation of Range: The range tells us that the tree heights in the forest vary by 45 feet. This tells us that there is a fairly wide variation in the heights of the trees in the forest showing a significant spread between the highest and lowest heights of trees.

  • To find the IQR, begin by putting the data in order.
  • Find the middle of the data set to split the data into two halves.
  • Using the lower half, find the middle value - Q1
  • Using the upper half, find the middle value - Q3
  • To find the IQR, subtract Q1 from Q3.
    • 85-60=25

IQR of Heights = 25 feet

Interpretation: The IQR tells us that there is a 25 feet difference between the heights of the trees in the middle 50% of measured trees in the data set. This means that 50% of the trees heights will be within 25 feet or less than each other.


Conlusion

Each of these measures provides a different perspective on the data. The mean offers an overall average, the median indicates the central value, and the mode highlights the most frequent occurrence. The choice of which measure to use depends on the nature of the data and the specific question being addressed. Both the range and the IQR provide insights into the variability of a data set, but they do so in different ways. The range considers the extremes of the data, while the IQR provides a more nuanced view by focusing on the central spread, making it particularly useful in data sets with outliers.
