Categorical Data: Data that can be grouped into categories based on shared characteristics or qualities.

Circle Graph: A visual representation of data in the form of a circle divided into sections, showing parts of a whole.

Percentage: A way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100.

Population: The entire group of individuals or items being studied or surveyed.

Predictions: An estimate about what might happen in the future based on data or trends.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to read and interpret data displayed in circle graphs. Circle graphs, also known as pie charts, are a great way to represent parts of a whole using percentages. By understanding proportions and percentages, you can use circle graphs to draw meaningful conclusions. You will learn to compare categories, identify the largest or smallest groups, and calculate how much or how many items are in each category based on the total.


What is a Circle Graph?

A circle graph, also known as a pie chart, is a way to show information as parts of a whole. It looks like a circle divided into slices, where each slice represents a category. The size of each slice shows how big or small that category is compared to the whole.

Each slice in a circle graph is labeled with either a percentage, a number, or both. The percentages always add up to 100% because the whole circle represents the entire group or total.

Circle graphs are useful when you want to compare different parts of a whole. They make it easy to see which category is the biggest, the smallest, or if any are about the same size.



Example 1

Below is a circle graph showing the favorite school subjects of 400 students.

Each slice of the graph represents a percentage of students who selected that subject.

Answer the following questions using the information above.

  1. Which subject is the most popular?
  2. How many students selected Art as their favorite subject?
  3. How many more students preferred Math over Science?
  4. Which two subjects were the least popular?

Solution:

  1. To find the most popular subject, we look for the subject that has the largest slice on the circle and the largest percentage.
    • The most popular subject is Art.
  2. To find how many students selected Art as their favorite, we take the percentage times the number of students in the class.
    • \(30\%\;of\;400=0.30\cdot400=120\)
    • 120 students choose Art as their favorite subject.
  3. To find how many more students preferred Math over Science, we need to find how many preferred math. Then find how many preferred science. Then we can subtract the two.
    • Preferred Math \(=25\%\;of\;400=0.25\cdot400=100\)
    • Preferred Science \(=20\%\;of\;400=0.20\cdot400=80\)
    • \(100-80=20\implies20\) students preferred Math over Science.
  4. To find which two subjects were the least popular, we look for the two subjects that have the smallest slice and the smallest percentages.
    • History and English were the least popular subjects.



Example 2

The circle graph below shows how a group of people spent 600 total minutes on household activities in a day.

Use the information above to answer the following questions.

  1. Which activity took the most time?
  2. How many minutes were spent on Cooking?
  3. If you combined the time for Exercising and Reading, how many minutes would that be?
  4. Which activity took less time: Social Media or Watching TV? By how much?

Solution:

  1. To find which activity took the most time, we look for the largest slice with the greatest percentage.
    • The most time was spent on Reading.
  2. To find how many minutes were spent on cooking, we will multiply the percentage times total minutes accounted for.
    • \(15\%\;of\;600=0.15\cdot600=90\)
    • People spent 90 minutes on cooking.
  3. To find how many minutes were spent on Exercising and Reading, we can first add together the percentages. Then take that of the total minutes.
    • Reading \(=22\%\) and Exercising \(=20\%\). Together they make up \(42\%\).
    • Minutes Spent on Both \(=42\%\;of\;600=0.42\cdot600=252\)
    • People spent 252 minutes on Exercising and Reading.
  4. To determine which activity took less time and by how much, we first compare the percentages of Social Media and Watching TV. Then we calculate the minutes for each and subtract them.
    • Social Media \(=17\%\) and Watching TV \(=18\%\)
    • Social Media \(=17\%\;of\;600=0.17\cdot600=102\) minutes
    • Watching TV \(=18\%\;of\;600=0.18\cdot600=108\) minutes
    • 6 less minutes was spent on Social Media than Watching TV.


Conclusion

Circle graphs are a powerful tool for visualizing data. By analyzing the proportions and percentages in each slice, you can quickly understand and compare categories. Whether you're examining student preferences or household activities, learning to read and interpret these graphs will help you make informed decisions and predictions. Keep practicing, and you'll master the art of interpreting circle graphs!
