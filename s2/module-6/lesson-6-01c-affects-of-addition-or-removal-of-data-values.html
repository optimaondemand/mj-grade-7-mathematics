📚 VOCABULARY

Data: Information collected in the form of numbers or observations, often used for analysis. Example: Heights of students in a class.

Interquartile Range (IQR): A measure of how spread out the middle half of a data set is. It is the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1). Example: If Q1 is 25 and Q3 is 75, the IQR is \(75 - 25 = 50\).

Mean: The average of a set of numbers, found by adding them all up and then dividing by the number of numbers. Example: The mean of 2, 4, and 6 is \(\frac{(2+4+6)}{3} = 4\).

Measures of Center: Statistical values that represent the middle or center of a data set. Common measures of center include the mean, median, and mode.

Measures of Variation: Statistical values that describe the spread or dispersion of a data set. Common measures of variation include range and interquartile range.

Median: The middle value in a set of numbers when they are arranged in order. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers. Example: The median of 3, 5, 7, 9 is 6.

Mode: The number that appears most frequently in a data set. A data set can have more than one mode or no mode at all. Example: The mode of 2, 2, 3, 5, 7 is 2.

Outlier: A value in a data set that is much higher or lower than the other values. Outliers can affect the mean and other statistical measures. Example: In the data set 3, 4, 5, 45, 6, 45 is an outlier.

Quartiles: Values that divide a data set into four equal parts. The first quartile (Q1) is the median of the lower half, and the third quartile (Q3) is the median of the upper half.

Range: The difference between the highest and lowest values in a data set. It gives an idea of how spread out the data is. Example: The range of 3, 7, 10 is \(10 - 3 = 7\).

Skewed Data: Data that is not symmetrically distributed. It can be skewed left (longer tail on the left side) or right (longer tail on the right side). Skewed data can affect the mean and median.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how adding a data value to a set of data or removing one of the data values affects the statistical measures for the data set. We will explore why these measures may or may not be affected and what that means in terms of the overall interpretation of the data set. We have been exploring what the measures of variation and center are and how to calculate them for a set of data. We will continue to look at those measures and how they are affected by a change in the set of data.


Effects on Measures of Center

  • Mean: Adding or removing a value can change the mean (average). If the new value is higher or lower than the current mean, it will pull the mean up or down.
  • Median: The median may or may not change, depending on where the added or removed value falls in relation to the other values.
  • Mode: The mode changes only if the frequency of the most common value is affected.


Effects on Measures of Variation

  • Range: Adding a value can increase the range if it's outside the current range. Removing a value can decrease the range if it's the highest or lowest value.
  • IQR: The IQR is less likely to change significantly unless the added or removed value is near the first or third quartile.



Example 1

Original data set \(\{5,7,9,11,13\}\)

What are the effects of adding the value \(10\) to the data set?

Solution:

  • The original range of the data set is \(13-5 = 8\)
  • Since \(10\) is within the minimum and maximum of the original data set, adding this value will have NO effect on the range.
  • The original mean is \(9\).
  • Since \(10\) is higher than the original mean, it will increase the value of the mean for the new set of data.
    • Since \(10\) is relatively close to the original mean, it will only increase the value of the mean slightly.
  • New mean \(=\frac{5+7+9+11+13+10}{6}=\frac{55}{6}=9.16666... \approx 9.17\)



Example 2

Original data set \(\{4,5,6.5,7,5.5,4.3,7.1,6.6,7.4,5.2\}\)

Range \(=7.4-4=3.4\)

Mean \(=\frac{4+5+6.5+7+5.5+4.3+7.1+6.6+7.4+5.2}{10}=\frac{58.6}{10}=5.86\)

What are the effects of adding the value \(2\) to the data set?

Solution:

  • Since the new value is less than the minimum of the original set, the range will DECREASE.
    • New Range \(=7.4-2=5.4\)
  • Since the value is significantly less than the original mean, the mean will also decrease.
    • New Mean \(\frac{4+5+6.5+7+5.5+4.3+7.1+6.6+7.4+5.2+3}{11}=\frac{61.6}{11}=5.6\)



Example 3

Original data set \(\{4,5,6.5,7,5.5,4.3,7.1,6.6,7.4,5.2\}\).

What are the effects of removing the data value \(5.5\) from the data set?

Solution:

Let's first look at the original measures of center and variation for the data set.

  • Measures of Center
    • Mean \(=\frac{4+5+6.5+7+5.5+4.3+7.1+6.6+7.4+5.2}{10}=\frac{58.6}{10}=5.86\)
    • Median \(=4,4.3,5,5.2,5.5\mid6.5,6.6,7,7.1,7.4 \implies \frac{5.5+6.5}{2}=6\)
  • Measures of Variation
    • Range \(=7.4-4=3.4\)
    • Interquartile Range \(=7-5=2\)

Now, let's look at the new measures of center and variation after removing \(5.5\) from the data set.

New data set \(\{4,5,6.5,7,4.3,7.1,6.6,7.4,5.2\}\)

  • New Measures of Center
    • Mean \(=\frac{4+5+6.5+7+4.3+7.1+6.6+7.4+5.2}{9}=\frac{53.1}{9}=5.9\)
      • Because we removed a value that was less than the original mean, the new mean increased in value.
      • If we had removed a value greater than the original mean, the value would have decreased.
    • Median \(=4,4.3,5,5.2,\boxed{6.5},6.6,7,7.1,7.4=6.5\)
      • Similar to the effects on the mean, removing a value less than the median causes the value of the new median to increase.
  • New Measures of Variation
    • Range \(=7.4-4=3.4\)
      • The range is unaffected since the value removed was neither the minimum nor maximum.
      • The range is calculated using these values. Therefore, unless you are removing one of those values, the range will be unaffected.
    • Interquartile Range \(=7-5=2\)
      • In this situation, the IQR was unaffected since a value close to the median was removed.
      • Removing a value can sometimes affect the IQR.
      • The IQR is less likely to change significantly unless the added or removed value is near the first or third quartile.


Conclusion

Adding or removing values from a data set can significantly impact the measures of center and variation. Understanding these changes helps us accurately interpret data and make informed decisions based on our analysis. Remember, the impact depends on how the new or removed value compares to the existing data set.
