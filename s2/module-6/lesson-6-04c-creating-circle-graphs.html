💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to construct a circle graph using data from real-world situations. A circle graph, or pie chart, is a great way to visualize parts of a whole. You will learn how to calculate percentages for each category in a data table and then assign those categories to segments in a pre-drawn circle graph. By the end of this lesson, you’ll be able to label a circle graph with category names and percentages.


What is a Circle Graph?

A circle graph, also known as a pie chart, is a way to show information as parts of a whole. It looks like a circle divided into slices, where each slice represents a category. The size of each slice shows how big or small that category is compared to the whole.

Each slice in a circle graph is labeled with either a percentage, a number, or both. The percentages always add up to 100% because the whole circle represents the entire group or total.

Circle graphs are useful when you want to compare different parts of a whole. They make it easy to see which category is the biggest, the smallest, or if any are about the same size.


Steps to Construct a Circle Graph

  1. Understand the data: Look at the table of data and identify the total.
  2. Calculate percentages: Divide the count for each category by the total and multiply by 100 to find the percentage for each category.
  3. Match percentages to segments: Use the pre-drawn circle graph segments and decide which category fits into each one based on the percentages.
  4. Label the graph: Write the category name and its percentage inside or near each segment.



Example 1

A group of 60 students was asked about their favorite ice cream flavor.

Flavor Number of Students
Chocolate 24
Vanilla 18
Strawberry 12
Mint Chip 6

Use data in the table to calculate the percentages for each category and label the segments in the graph to match.

Solution:

Step 1: Calculate the Percentages for Each Category - Total all up all students to find the total number students.

  • Chocolate \(=\frac{24}{60}=0.4\cdot100=40\%\)
  • Vanilla \(=\frac{18}{60}=0.3\cdot100=30\%\)
  • Strawberry \(=\frac{12}{60}=0.2\cdot100=20\%\)
  • Mint Chip \(=\frac{6}{60}=0.1\cdot100=10\%\)

Step 2: Identify which category will match each segment in the graph.

  • The largest percentage Chocolate will go with the largest segment on the graph.
    • We will label the orange segment Chocolate-40%
  • The second largest Vanilla will go with the segment just smaller than the largest piece.
    • We will label the purple segment Vanilla-30%
  • The third category Strawberry goes with the second smallest segment.
    • We will label the green segment Strawberry-20%
  • The last one Mint Chip will go with the smallest segment.
    • We will label the blue segment Mint Chip-10%

Step 3: Finally label the circle graph.

Conclusion

Constructing a circle graph is a simple way to visualize data and understand how different parts relate to the whole. By calculating percentages and labeling segments correctly, you can effectively represent data in a clear and organized way. Keep practicing with different data sets, and soon you’ll master the art of creating and interpreting circle graphs!
