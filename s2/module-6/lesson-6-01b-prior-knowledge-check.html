📖 PRIOR KNOWLEDGE

In a previous grade, you learned how to calculate the mean, median, and mode of a set of data. As we get ready to explore the measures of center and their meaning in terms of the data, it is important to review how to find the measures of center; mean, median, and mode. Being able to calculate statistical measures is the first step to understanding what they mean for the data set and their graphical representations.


What is the Mean?

The mean is the average of a set of numbers. It's calculated by adding up all the numbers and then dividing by the number of numbers.

How to Calculate the Mean:

  1. Add up all the numbers in your data set.
  2. Divide the total by the number of numbers in the set.


What is the Median?

The median is the middle value in a set of numbers when they are arranged in order. If there's an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers.

How to Calculate the Median:

  1. Arrange the numbers in numerical order.
  2. Find the middle number. If there are two middle numbers, find their average.


What is the Mode?

The mode is the number that appears most frequently in a data set. A set of data may have one mode, more than one mode, or no mode at all.

How to Find the Mode:

  1. Look for the number or numbers that appear most frequently.
  2. If there is more than one number that appears most frequently, list all numbers.

Conclusion

Understanding how to calculate the mean, median, and mode gives you powerful tools to analyze and interpret data.
