📝 ASSIGNMENT

Objective:

In this project, you will explore two different types of data:

  1. Numerical Data (data that consists of numbers, such as test scores, ages, or heights).
  2. Categorical Data (data that consists of groups, such as favorite sports, types of pets, or preferred ice cream flavors).

Task:

  • Choose one numerical data set and one categorical data set
  • Select the best graphical representation for each type of data
  • Create graphs to represent the data
  • Analyze the data and describe patterns, trends, and key statistics
  • Compile your work into a document or presentation
📋 PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS

Step 1: Choose Your Data Sets

  1. A numerical data set - Choose one set of data from the given list on this
📋 PDF FOR YOUR NUMERICAL DATA.
  • A categorical data set - Choose one set of data from the given list on this
    • 📋 PDF FOR YOUR CATEGORICAL DATA.


    Step 2: Choose the Right Graphical Representation

    • For numerical data, you may choose a line plot, histogram, stem-and-leaf plot, or box plot.
    • For categorical data, you may choose a bar graph or circle graph (pie chart).

    Think about which type of graph will best represent the data and make trends easy to see. Refer back to the lesson page to see what type is best for what you want to display.


    Step 3: Create Your Graphs

    • Use graphing tools such as graph paper, Google Sheets, Excel, or an online graph creator.
    • Make sure your graph has:
      • A title that describes what the data represents
      • Labels for the categories or numerical values
      • A clear and accurate representation of the data


    Step 4: Analyze Your Data

    For categorical data, describe the trends:

    • Which category is the most popular?
    • Which category is the least popular?
    • Are there any categories with similar frequencies?
    • Add 3 other questions you come up with on your own. You can refer back to previous assignments to help think of some.

    Each question and answer will be worth a point on this page.

    For numerical data, describe the key features:

    • Identify gaps (places where no data appears).
    • Identify peaks (most frequently occurring values).
    • Describe skewness (is the data evenly spread, or is it concentrated on one side?).
    • Look for outliers (values that are much higher or lower than the rest).


    Step 5: Calculate and Show Statistical Measures

    For numerical data, calculate and display:

    • Mean (average)
    • Median (middle value)
    • Mode (most common value)
    • Range (difference between highest and lowest values)
    • Interquartile Range (IQR) if using a box plot

    For categorical data, state:

    • Mode (most common category)
    • Percentages (if using a circle graph, show how each category compares to the total)


    Step 6: Create a Final Document or Presentation

    You will create a Google Slides, PowerPoint, or Word/Google Doc that includes:

    1. Title Page – Include your name, project title, and date.
    2. Data Sets – Show the numerical and categorical data you collected.
    3. Graphical Representations – Display the graphs you created for each data set.
    4. Data Analysis – Explain trends, gaps, peaks, skewness, or other observations.
    5. Statistical Calculations – Show your work for mean, median, mode, and other relevant statistics.
    6. Conclusion – Summarize what the data tells you and why your chosen graphs were useful.
    📋 EXPECTATIONS & GRADING CRITERIA

    Your project will be graded on:
    ✅ Completeness – Did you include all required elements?
    ✅ Accuracy – Are your graphs correctly labeled and data accurately represented?
    ✅ Analysis – Did you describe trends, gaps, peaks, and skewness clearly?
    ✅ Statistical Calculations – Are your calculations correct and clearly shown?
    ✅ Presentation & Organization – Is your work neatly presented with clear explanations?

    Each step above will account for 5 points towards the total. Points will be awarded for the above criteria.
