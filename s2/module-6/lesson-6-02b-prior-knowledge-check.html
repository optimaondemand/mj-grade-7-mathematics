In a previous lesson, you learned what the measures of variation are, how to calculate them, and what they mean in terms of the context. As we get ready to explore appropriate measures of variation for given data sets, it is important that we review what the measures of variation are and how to calculate them.


What is Range?

The range is a measure of variation that shows the difference between the highest and lowest values in a data set. It gives us a basic idea of how spread out the data is. It's like measuring the distance from the lowest point to the highest point in your data.


What is Interquartile Range (IQR)?

The interquartile range is a measure of variation that describes the range of the middle half of a data set. It is calculated by finding the difference between the first quartile (Q1) and the third quartile (Q3).



How to Find the Range:

  1. Identify the highest (maximum) and lowest (minimum) values in your data set.
  2. Subtract the minimum value from the maximum value.
  3. The result is the range.

Interpreting the Meaning of the Range

The range tells you about the spread or variability in your data set. A larger range means there is a wider spread between the lowest and highest values, indicating more variability. Conversely, a smaller range suggests that the data points are closer together, indicating less variability. When interpreting the range, think about what this spread means in the context of your data. For example, in a classroom test score scenario, a large range could indicate that there's a big difference in understanding or performance levels among the students.

How to Find the IQR:

  1. Arrange the data in ascending order.
  2. Find the median (middle value) of the data set.
  3. Divide the data into two halves at the median.
  4. Find the median of each half. These are Q1 and Q3.
  5. Subtract Q1 from Q3: IQR = Q3 - Q1.

Interpreting the Meaning of the Interquartile Range

The interquartile range (IQR) tells us about the spread of the middle 50% of the data. It is a measure of variability that indicates the range within which the central half of the data lies.

  • A smaller IQR suggests that the middle 50% of the data is closely grouped together.
  • A larger IQR indicates more spread out data in the middle 50%, suggesting greater variability in the data set.
  • The IQR is particularly useful because it is not affected by outliers or extreme values, making it a reliable measure of spread for skewed distributions.


Example 3

A teacher collected the test courses of her 18 students on a math test. The data set shows the test scores for her students.

  • Begin by putting the data set in order from smallest to largest.
  • Find the middle of the set of data to split the data in half.
  • Identify the middle of the lower half of the data - Q1
    • We find that Q1 = 70
  • Identify the middle of the upper half of the data - Q3
    • We find that Q3 = 86
  • To find the interquartile range (IQR), subtract Q1 from Q3.
    • IQR = Q3 - Q1 = 86 - 70 = 16

Interpretation: The IQR tells us that the middle 50% of the test scores ranged from 70 to 86, a spread of 16 points. This is a somewhat significant spread for the middle 50% of the test scores. This suggests a significant variability in the whole data set.

 

Conclusion

Understanding the range and interquartile range helps us to grasp how varied or consistent a set of data is. The range gives us a quick view of the overall spread, while the IQR focuses on the spread of the central portion of the data, providing a clearer picture in cases where there are extreme values or outliers. These measures are crucial in fields like meteorology, sports, education, and many more, helping us make sense of the world through data.
