📚 VOCABULARY

Bar Graph: A chart that uses bars to show comparisons among categories of data. Each bar represents a category of data, and the height or length of the bar shows the value of that category.

Box Plot: Displays the distribution of data based on five summary numbers: the minimum, the first quartile (Q1), the median, the third quartile (Q3), and the maximum. It's useful for showing the median, quartiles, and outliers at a glance.

Histogram: Similar to a bar graph but is used for numerical data that are grouped into bins or intervals. It shows how many data points fall into each interval, allowing you to see the distribution of the data.

Interquartile Range (IQR): Measures the spread of the middle 50% of data points. It is calculated as the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1), showing the range of the central half of a dataset.

Line Plot: Shows data along a number line, with each data point marked by an 'x' or dot. It's useful for showing changes over time or distribution of small sets of data.

Mean: The average of a set of numbers, found by adding all the numbers together and then dividing by the count of those numbers.

Measures of Center: Gives us an idea of the middle or central tendency of a dataset. The most common measures of center are the mean and the median.

Measures of Variation: Describes the spread or dispersion of a set of data points. Common measures include the range, interquartile range (IQR), and standard deviation.

Median: The middle value of a dataset when it is ordered from smallest to largest. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers.

Numerical Data: Quantities or numbers that can be counted or measured. They can be used for mathematical calculations and statistical analysis.

Outlier: A data point that is significantly different from the other data points in a dataset. It can be much higher or lower than the rest of the data.

Quartiles: Values that divide a dataset into four equal parts. The first quartile (Q1) is the median of the lower half of the dataset, the second quartile (Q2) is the median of the dataset, and the third quartile (Q3) is the median of the upper half of the dataset.

Range: A measure of variation that shows the difference between the highest and lowest values in a dataset.

Stem-and-Leaf Plot: A  method of displaying numerical data in an organized way, showing the distribution of data. Each number is divided into a stem (the leading digit(s)) and a leaf (the last digit). It's useful for observing the shape of a distribution while retaining the original data values.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will be exploring how to compare two sets of numerical data. We'll focus on using two measures of center—mean and median—and two measures of variation—range and interquartile range (IQR). By understanding these concepts, you'll be able to analyze and draw conclusions about different data sets, which is a valuable skill in math and in real life.

Let's begin by reminding ourselves what the measures of center and measures of variation are. Then we will watch a video on comparing two data sets about the heights of two different sets of trees from two different forests.


Measures of Center

  1. Mean: The mean is the average of a data set. You calculate it by adding all the numbers in the data set and then dividing by the number of values.
  2. Median: The median is the middle value of a data set when it is ordered from least to greatest. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers.


Measures of Variation

  1. Range: The range shows the spread of a data set. It's the difference between the highest and lowest values.
  2. Interquartile Range (IQR): The IQR measures the middle spread of the data. It's the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1). This tells us where the central 50% of the values lie.



Comparing Data Sets

When comparing two data sets, look at both the measures of center and variation. A higher mean or median indicates a higher overall value, while a larger range or IQR indicates more variability among the data. By examining these measures, you can infer characteristics about the populations from which the data sets are drawn.

Drawing Conclusions

To compare two sets of data:

  1. Calculate the mean and median to understand the center of the data.
  2. Calculate the range and IQR to understand the spread and consistency.
  3. Analyze these measures to draw conclusions about the populations.


Example 1

Imagine two classrooms, A and B, took a math test. Here are their scores:

  • Classroom A: \(85, 92, 88, 74, 91, 77, 83, 89, 95, 78\)
  • Classroom B: \(82, 80, 79, 81, 83, 82, 80, 84, 79, 81\)

Comparing Measures of Center

  • Mean of A \(=\frac{85+92+88+74+91+77+83+89+95+78}{10}=\frac{852}{10}=85.2\)

  • Median of A \(=74,77,78,83,\boxed{85,88},89,91,92,95 \implies \frac{85+88}{2}=86.5\)

  • Mean of B \(=\frac{82+80+79+81+83+82+80+84+79+81}{10}=\frac{811}{10}=81.1\)

  • Median of B \(=79,79,80,80,\boxed{81,81},82,82,83,84 \implies 81\)

You may notice that the scores in class A are overall higher than Class B. The average and typical score from class A is \(85.2\) and \(86.5\). Whereas in Class B the average is \(81.1\) and the typical is around \(81\). We can conclude that on average, Class A performed better on the test than Class B.


Comparing Measures of Variation

  • Range of A \(=95-74=21\)

  • IQR of A \(=74,77,\boxed{78},83,85,\mid85,89,\boxed{91},92,95 \implies Q1=78 \;and \;Q3=91 \implies IQR=91-78=13\)

For Class A, the scores ranged about \(21\) points between the highest and the lowest. The middle 50% had a range of \(13\) points.

  • Range of B \(=84-79=5\)

  • IQR of B \(=79,79,\boxed{80},80,81m\mid81,82,\boxed{82},83,84 \implies Q1=80 \;and \;Q3=82 \implies IQR=82-80=2\)

For Class B, the scores ranged about \(5\) points from the highest to the lowest and the middle 50% had a range of only \(2\) points.

Conclusion for Test Scores: Classroom A has a higher mean and median, indicating that, on average, students in Classroom A scored higher on the math test than those in Classroom B. However, Classroom A also has a larger range and IQR, suggesting greater variability in scores. This means that while Classroom A generally performed better, their scores were more spread out, indicating differences in individual performances.


Example 2

Students in two study groups, X and Y, reported the following hours spent on homework in a week:

  • Group X: \(5.0, 7.5, 6.0, 5.5, 4.5, 6.5, 5.0, 7.0, 6.5, 5.5\)
  • Group Y: \(4.0, 3.5, 4.5, 4.0, 3.0, 4.5, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 3.5\)

Comparing Measures of Center

  • Mean of X \(=\frac{5+7.5+6+5.5+4.5+6.5+5+7+6.5+5.5}{10}=\frac{59}{10}=5.9\)

  • Median of X \(=4.5,5,5,5.5,\boxed{5.5,6},6.5,6.5,7,7.5=\frac{5.5+6}{2}=5.75\)

  • Mean of Y \(=\frac{4+3.5+4.5+4+3+4.5+3.5+4+4.5+3.5}{10}=\frac{39}{10}=3.9\)

  • Median of Y \(=3,3.5,3.5,3.5,\boxed{4,4},4,4.5,4.5,4.5=4\)

The average number of hours spent on homework in Group X was \(5.405\) whereas the average was only \(3.9\) hours for Group Y. We can conclude that on average Group Y spent less time on homework. We could infer that they have different teachers or are in different grades where the homework load is less demanding. The typical number of hours spent on homework for Group X was \(5.75\), but only \(4\) in Group Y.


Comparing Measures of Variation

  • Range of X \(=7.5-4.5=3\)

  • IQR of X \(=4.5,5,\boxed{5},5.5,5.5,\mid6,6.5,\boxed{6.5},7,7.5 \implies Q1=5 \;and \;Q3=6.5 \implies IQR=6.5-5=1.5\)

The range of hours spent on homework only varied by about \(3\) hours for the whole group and only \(1.5\) hours for the middle 50%. Most of the students in the group were spending nearly the same amount of time working on homework and there were not any outliers of spending significantly less or more time than other students.

  • Range of Y \(=4.5-3=1.5\)

  • IQR of Y \(=3,3.5,\boxed{3.5},3.5,4,\mid4,4,\boxed{4.5},4.5,4.5 \implies Q1=3.5 \;and \;Q3=4.5 \implies IQR=4.5-3.5=1\)

Group Y was even more consistent in the number of hours spent on homework which could be inferred that they all felt the same comfortability level in their studies.

Conclusion for Homework Hours: Group X, on average, spends more hours on homework per week than Group Y, as shown by both the higher mean and median. The range and IQR of Group X are also higher, indicating that there's more variability in the number of hours Group X students dedicate to homework. In contrast, Group Y's hours are more consistent but lower overall.


Conclusion

By comparing the measures of center and variation, we can understand not just the "average" behavior of each group but also how consistent or varied the data within each group is. This method of analysis can be applied to a wide range of real-world situations, helping us make informed conclusions about different populations. Remember, the key is to look at both the center and the spread to get a full picture of the data.
