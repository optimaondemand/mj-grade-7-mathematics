📚 VOCABULARY

Data: Information collected in the form of numbers or observations, often used for analysis. Example: Heights of students in a class.

Interquartile Range (IQR): A measure of how spread out the middle half of a data set is. It is the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1). Example: If Q1 is 25 and Q3 is 75, the IQR is \(75 - 25 = 50\).

Mean: The average of a set of numbers, found by adding them all up and then dividing by the number of numbers. Example: The mean of 2, 4, and 6 is \(\frac{(2+4+6)}{3} = 4\).

Measures of Center: Statistical values that represent the middle or center of a data set. Common measures of center include the mean, median, and mode.

Measures of Variation: Statistical values that describe the spread or dispersion of a data set. Common measures of variation include range and interquartile range.

Median: The middle value in a set of numbers when they are arranged in order. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers. Example: The median of 3, 5, 7, 9 is 6.

Mode: The number that appears most frequently in a data set. A data set can have more than one mode or no mode at all. Example: The mode of 2, 2, 3, 5, 7 is 2.

Outlier: A value in a data set that is much higher or lower than the other values. Outliers can affect the mean and other statistical measures. Example: In the data set 3, 4, 5, 45, 6, 45 is an outlier.

Quartiles: Values that divide a data set into four equal parts. The first quartile (Q1) is the median of the lower half, and the third quartile (Q3) is the median of the upper half.

Range: The difference between the highest and lowest values in a data set. It gives an idea of how spread out the data is. Example: The range of 3, 7, 10 is \(10 - 3 = 7\).

Skewed Data: Data that is not symmetrically distributed. It can be skewed left (longer tail on the left side) or right (longer tail on the right side). Skewed data can affect the mean and median.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore three important statistical concepts; mean, median, and mode. These statistical measures are called measures of center. These measures of center help us to understand the central point or typical value in a set of data. Today, we will explore these statistical measures using a set of data and interpret their meaning in terms of that data set. In a later lesson, we will explore what these measures mean to a graphical representation and what that data plot will look like.


What are the Measures of Center?

Measures of center give us a single value that represents the middle or typical value of a data set. The three main measures of center are:

  1. Mean: The average of all the values.
  2. Median: The middle value when the data is arranged in order.
  3. Mode: The most frequently occurring value.



What is the Mean?

The mean is the average of a set of numbers. It's calculated by adding up all the numbers and then dividing by the number of numbers. It represents the average value.


How to Calculate the Mean:

  1. Add up all the numbers in your data set.
  2. Divide the total by the number of numbers in the set.

Interpretation:

  • The mean gives you a general idea of where the center of the data lies.
  • It is best used when the data is symmetrically distributed and has no outliers, as outliers can skew the mean.
  • It is useful for understanding the overall level of a variable in the data set, like the average score, average temperature, etc.


Example 1

A teacher records the number of students present each day over two weeks. The data set shows the number of students present for the 10 school days.

\(\{22,24,23,25,22,24,23,25,26,22\}\)

Find and interpret the mean for the data set. Round to the nearest hundredth if necessary.

  • To find the mean, add all the values.
    • \(22+24+23+25+22+24+23+25+26+22=236\)
  • Now, divide by the number of values; \(14\).
    • \(\frac{236}{10}=23.6\)

Mean Attendance = \(23.6\) students

Interpretation: The average attendance is around \(23.6\) students or between \(23\) and \(24\) students.


Example 2

A biologist measures the heights of trees in a forest. The data set shown represents the heights, in feet (ft), of the measured trees.

\(\{50,54,60,66,70,83,80,85,90,67,72,81,58,60\}\)

Find and interpret the mean for the data set. Round to the nearest hundredth if necessary.

  • To find the mean, add all the values.
    • \(50+54+60+66+70+83+80+85+90+67+72+81+58+60=976\)
  • Divide by the number of values in the set.
    • \(\frac{976}{14}=69.71428571...\)

Mean Height = \(69.71 ft\)

Interpretation: The average height of a tree in that forest is around \(69.71 ft\).



What is the Median?

The median is the middle value in a set of numbers when they are arranged in order. If there's an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers.


How to Calculate the Median:

  1. Arrange the numbers in numerical order.
  2. Find the middle number. If there are two middle numbers, find their average.

Interpretation:

  • The median divides the data set into two equal halves.
  • It is less affected by outliers or skewed data compared to the mean.
  • The median is a good indicator of the central tendency, especially in skewed distributions, as it represents a value that is central to the data set.

What is the Mode?

The mode is the number that appears most frequently in a data set. A set of data may have one mode, more than one mode, or no mode at all.


How to Find the Mode:

  1. Look for the number or numbers that appear most frequently.

Interpretation:

  • The mode indicates the most common value or category in the data set.
  • It is particularly useful for categorical data where mean and median may not be applicable.
  • In a data set with a clear mode (or modes), it highlights the value(s) that occur most often, which can be significant in understanding trends or preferences.

Conclusion

Each of these measures of center – mean, median, and mode – provides a different perspective on the data. The mean offers an overall average, the median provides a central value unaffected by extremes, and the mode shows the most common occurrence. The choice of which measure to use and interpret depends on the nature of the data and the specific question you are trying to answer. Understanding the mean, median, and mode helps us to summarize and interpret data effectively. These measures of center can provide insights into trends, common values, and typical behaviors in various real-world situations. Practice calculating these measures with different data sets to enhance your data analysis skills!
