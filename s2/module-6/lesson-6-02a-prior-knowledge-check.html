As we get ready to explore appropriate measures of center for given data sets, it is important to review what measures of center are and how to calculate them.


What are the Measures of Center?

Measures of center give us a single value that represents the middle or typical value of a data set. The three main measures of center are:

  1. Mean: The average of all the values.
  2. Median: The middle value when the data is arranged in order.


What is the Mean?

The mean is the average of a set of numbers. It's calculated by adding up all the numbers and then dividing by the number of numbers. It represents the average value.


How to Calculate the Mean:

  1. Add up all the numbers in your data set.
  2. Divide the total by the number of numbers in the set.

Interpretation:

  • The mean gives you a general idea of where the center of the data lies.
  • It is best used when the data is symmetrically distributed and has no outliers, as outliers can skew the mean.
  • It is useful for understanding the overall level of a variable in the data set, like the average score, average temperature, etc.

A teacher records the number of students present each day over two weeks. The data set shows the number of students present for the 10 school days.

  • To find the mean, add all the values.
  • Now, divide by the number of values; 14.

Mean Attendance = 23.6 students

Interpretation: The average attendance is around 23.6 students or between 23 and 24 students.

 

What is the Median?

The median is the middle value in a set of numbers when they are arranged in order. If there's an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers.


How to Calculate the Median:

  1. Arrange the numbers in numerical order.
  2. Find the middle number. If there are two middle numbers, find their average.

Interpretation:

  • The median divides the data set into two equal halves.
  • It is less affected by outliers or skewed data compared to the mean.
  • The median is a good indicator of the central tendency, especially in skewed distributions, as it represents a value that is central to the data set.

Conclusion

Each of these measures of center – mean and median – provides a different perspective on the data. The mean offers an overall average and the median provides a central value unaffected by extremes. The choice of which measure to use and interpret depends on the nature of the data and the specific question you are trying to answer. Understanding the mean and median helps us to summarize and interpret data effectively. These measures of center can provide insights into trends, common values, and typical behaviors in various real-world situations.
