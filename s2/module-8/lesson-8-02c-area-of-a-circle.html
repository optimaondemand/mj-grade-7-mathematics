📚 VOCABULARY

Area: How much space is inside a shape. For example, if you have a square piece of land and each side is 10 feet long, the area would be 100 square feet because you multiply the length by the width (10 feet * 10 feet = 100 square feet). For shapes like circles, we have special formulas to figure out how much space is inside.

Circle: A round shape where every point on the edge is the same distance from the center. Imagine tying a string to a pencil. If you hold the end of the string at one point and draw around it, keeping the string tight, you'll make a circle. The point where you're holding the string is the center of the circle.

Circumference: The distance around a circle, like a fence that surrounds a yard. If you were to walk all the way around a circular garden, the path you walk would be the garden's circumference. It's like the perimeter, but for round shapes.

Diameter: A circle is a straight line that goes from one side of the circle to the other side, passing through the center. It's the longest distance you can measure across a circle. If you think of a pizza, the diameter is the length of the pizza when you cut it straight from one edge to the other, through the middle.

Pi (): A special number that helps us understand circles. Its value is about 3.14, but it actually goes on forever without repeating. Pi is super important because it relates the circumference of any circle to its diameter. No matter how big or small the circle is, if you divide the circumference by the diameter, you'll always get .

Radius: A circle is the distance from the center of the circle to any point on its edge. If the circle is a pizza, the radius is like a slice from the tip, where the pointy part is, to the crust straight across the middle. It's half the length of the diameter. So, if the diameter is the length of the whole pizza, the radius is the length of half the pizza.
