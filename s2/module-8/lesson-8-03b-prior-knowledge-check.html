What is a Ratio?

A ratio is a comparison of two quantities that tells us how many times one value contains another. It is expressed as two numbers separated by a colon (e.g., 1:2), the word "to" (e.g., 1 to 2), or as a fraction (e.g., 1/2).


Ways to Write Ratios

Writing ratios is easy once you know what you're comparing. Remember, a ratio always compares two quantities.

For example, there are 3 boys for every 2 girls in Tom's class. Here's how you can write them:

  • As a fraction: ​
  • With a colon: 3 : 2
  • Using the word "to": 3 boys to 2 girls or 3 boys = 2 girls


Writing Ratios

  1. Identify the Quantities: Determine the two items or quantities you want to compare.

  2. Order Matters: The order in which you write the numbers in a ratio is important. The first number refers to the first item mentioned, and the second number refers to the second item.

  3. Use the Same Units: Before writing a ratio, make sure both quantities are in the same units.

  4. Express the Ratio: Write the ratio using a colon, the word "to," or as a fraction.


Example 1

If a recipe calls for 2 cups of flour to 1 cup of sugar, what is the ratio of flour to sugar?

The ratio would be 2 to 1.

This can also be written as in a more mathematical representation.


If we decide to triple the recipe and use 6 cups of flour so we can serve more, how many cups of sugar would we need?

To find the cups of sugar, this is where understanding equivalent fractions is helpful.

If we multiply the cups of flour by 3 to get 6 when we triple the recipe, then we must do the same to the cups of sugar to keep the ratio equivalent.

We find that the recipe will need 3 cups of sugar when we triple it.

Example 2

The ratio of boys to girls in Mrs. Starling's class is 8:17. What does this ratio represent and what is the ratio of girls to total students in the class?

The ratio tells us that there are 8 boys to 17 girls in the class.

We can find the total number of students by adding 8 and 17 to find there are 25 students in Mrs. Starling's class.

We can use this information to find the ratio of girls to total students; 17:25.

The 8:17 represents a part-to-part ratio. We have the two parts of the whole.

The ratio 17:25 represents part-to-whole. We have the part representing the girls and the whole class.

We could also find that the ratio of boys to the whole class is 8:25.


Conclusion

Ratios are a powerful tool for comparing quantities and understanding their relationships. They are not just abstract numbers; they have practical applications that can help us make sense of the world. Whether you're cooking, crafting, or calculating, ratios are an essential part of problem-solving. Remember, practice makes perfect, so keep exploring ratios in different contexts! It is important we understand how to interpret, write, and use ratios as we get ready to explore ratio tables to explore part-to-part and part-to-part-to-whole ratios.
