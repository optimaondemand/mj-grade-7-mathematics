In a previous grade, you learned about basic shapes and how to measure them in various ways. One of the fundamental shapes you've encountered is the triangle, a figure that can be as simple as a piece of pie or as majestic as a pyramid. Triangles are everywhere, forming the building blocks of geometry. Today, we're going to revisit the triangle and focus on how to find its area, an essential skill in both math and real-life applications.


What is a Triangle?

A triangle is a polygon with three edges and three vertices. It's one of the simplest shapes in geometry, but it comes in many types, including equilateral (all sides and angles are equal), isosceles (two sides and angles are equal), scalene (all sides and angles are different), and right (one angle is 90 degrees). Despite their differences, all triangles share a common feature: they have three sides, and the sum of their internal angles always adds up to 180 degrees.

  1. Identify the base: The base is the side of the triangle that is perpendicular to the height. In this case, the base and height are clearly marked or drawn for you.
  2. Measure the height: The height is the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex. Since it's already drawn, simply note its measurement.
  3. Apply the formula: Use the formula for the area of a triangle by multiplying the length of the base by the height, and then dividing by two to find the area.
