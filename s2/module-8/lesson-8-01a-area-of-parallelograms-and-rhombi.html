📚 VOCABULARY

Area: The amount of space inside a shape. Imagine filling a shape with tiles; the area tells you how many tiles you'll need. It's usually measured in square units, like square meters.

Base: The bottom side. For some shapes, like triangles or trapezoids, any side can be considered the base when calculating area or other properties.

Composite Figure: A shape made up of two or more simpler shapes, such as rectangles, triangles, or circles. Think of it like a puzzle made from different pieces.

Decomposition: Breaking down a complex shape into simpler parts. It's like cutting up a cake into pieces so it's easier to share.

Height: The vertical distance from the base to the top. In some shapes like triangles, the height is the perpendicular line from the base to the opposite vertex or side.

Parallelogram: A four-sided shape where opposite sides are parallel and equal in length. Imagine a leaning rectangle where the top and bottom lines stay parallel.

Polygon: A flat shape with straight sides. Triangles, rectangles, and pentagons are all examples of polygons.

Quadrilateral: A four-sided polygon. Squares, rectangles, and rhombuses are all types of quadrilaterals.

Rectangle: A four-sided shape where every angle is a right angle (90 degrees), and opposite sides are equal in length. It's like a box or a TV screen.

Rhombus: A four-sided shape where all sides have equal length, and opposite sides are parallel. It looks like a diamond or a kite.

Triangle: A shape with three straight sides and three angles. Think of road signs or a slice of pie.

Trapezoid: A four-sided shape with at least one pair of parallel sides. Imagine a stretched or squished triangle with a flat top.

What is a Parallelogram?

A parallelogram is a type of quadrilateral, which is just a fancy name for a four-sided polygon. What makes a parallelogram special is that its opposite sides are parallel and equal in length. This property leads to other interesting characteristics, such as opposite angles being equal, and the diagonals bisecting each other.


Parts of a Parallelogram

  • Base: The base of a parallelogram is any of its sides. When calculating area, you can choose either pair of parallel sides as the base.
  • Height: The height (or altitude) is a perpendicular line segment from the base to the opposite side. Remember, the height is not the side length unless the parallelogram is a rectangle.
  • Parallel Sides: These are the pairs of sides that are parallel to each other. In a parallelogram, both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

What is a Rhombus?

A rhombus is a type of parallelogram where all four sides are of equal length. This definition gives it a distinct diamond shape. Though all sides are equal, it's important to note that the angles can vary, meaning not all rhombuses look exactly the same. However, they all share some common properties, such as opposite angles being equal and diagonals bisecting each other at right angles.

Diagonals in a Rhombus

Diagonals in a rhombus are the straight lines connecting opposite corners. In a rhombus, the diagonals have two key properties:

  1. They bisect each other at right angles (90 degrees). This means they cut each other exactly in half and form right angles where they cross.
  2. They bisect the angles of the rhombus. This means that each diagonal cuts the angles it connects into two equal parts.
